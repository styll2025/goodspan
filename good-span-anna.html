Example Good Span

Anna, 52

School administrator

Anna, you told us you'd like to sleep better, eat better and see your friends more, so that's where we're starting.

You already look after your health checks and make time to read, and we'll build on what already works.

This Good Span starts with

Two practices · Sleep · Nutrition · Connection · about 15 minutes a week

Where a small change could go a long way

Focus

Sleep

You'd like support with feeling more rested in the mornings.

Your bedtime varies by more than an hour during the week. A steadier rhythm, with some daylight soon after waking, can help mornings feel easier.

What’s already going well

  • Sleeping about 7 hours on most nights.
  • Keeping your bedroom dark and quiet.

Focus

Nutrition

You'd like support with eating more vegetables and fruit, and planning meals ahead.

You usually eat 1–2 portions of vegetables and fruit a day, and at least 5 are recommended. Small additions to meals you already eat can make a real difference.

What’s already going well

  • Cooking at home most evenings.
  • Keeping sugary drinks occasional.

Focus

Connection

You'd like support with seeing friends more often.

Busy weeks mean most meals are eaten alone. Sharing even one meal a week can help you feel more connected.

What’s already going well

  • Talking to your family every week.
  • Staying in touch with friends by message.

We build on these habits instead of asking her to start from scratch.

First two practices

The first steps for the month ahead, chosen around what matters to Anna and the time she has.

Sleep

01 · Morning daylight

Get some natural light outdoors soon after you wake up, for 5 minutes, 3 days a week.

Time

About 15 minutes a week.

Make it work for you

Step outside, for example with your morning drink or on your way out. Being outdoors matters more than what you do there, because daylight is far brighter than indoor lighting.

Why this practice

You told us mornings often feel hard. Daylight soon after waking helps set your body clock, so mornings feel easier.

What supports this: Tähkämö et al. (2019)

Nutrition

02 · Balanced plate

Add a portion of vegetables or fruit to one meal a day, 3 days a week.

Time

3 days a week. It fits into meals you already have, without setting extra time aside.

Make it work for you

Good choices include apples and pears, citrus fruits, leafy greens, broccoli, cabbage and salads.

Why this practice

You told us you want to eat more vegetables and fruit, and you usually eat 1–2 portions a day. At least 5 are recommended.

What supports this: Aune et al. (2017)

The six-month journey

A Good Span is a starting point, not a fixed prescription. Each month, Anna reflects on what’s working, adapts what isn’t and decides what comes next. New practices join one at a time, once the earlier ones feel familiar.

  1. Month 1 · Begin

    Get familiar with the first two practices and find ways to make them part of the week.

    About 15 minutes a week

  2. Month 2 · Build

    Keep the practices that are working and, when ready, add:

    • SleepScreen-free wind-downPut your phone away 30 minutes before bed on 3 nights a week.
    • ConnectionCalling instead of textingCall someone you would normally just text, for about 10 minutes, once a week.

    About 25 minutes a week in all

  3. Month 3 · Refine

    Review progress and adjust the practices to fit routines, priorities and experience. If it feels right, add:

    • SleepConsistent sleep timingChoose a regular bedtime and keep to it every weeknight, Monday to Friday.

    About 25 minutes a week in all, within the 1–2 hours you told us you have

  4. Months 4–5 · Deepen

    Explore the next practices in the focus areas, building on what’s been learned so far.

  5. Month 6 · Consolidate

    Identify the habits that matter most and make them easier to sustain beyond the six months.

  6. After six months · New Position

    Revisit the six dimensions to see how habits and priorities have evolved since the Starting Position, review the New Position with the Pilot and decide what to focus on next.

    Two possible next directions

    • Restful routines could help you build steadier sleep, with calmer evenings and brighter mornings.
    • Shared moments could help you keep friends and family part of your week, around meals, calls and time together.

    Or keep going with what’s working. Maintaining a habit that supports wellbeing is progress too.
