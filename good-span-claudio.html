Example Good Span

Claudio, 38

Architect and keen cyclist

Claudio, you told us you'd like to get fitter, sleep more regularly and drink a little less, so that's where we're starting.

You already ride most days, cook well and have a strong circle around you, and we'll build on what already works.

This Good Span starts with

Two practices · Movement · Sleep · Prevention · about 30 minutes a week

First two practices

The first steps for the month ahead, chosen around what matters to Claudio and the time he has.

Movement

01 · Short intervals

Do a short-interval session of about 30 minutes, including a warm-up, once a week.

Time

About 30 minutes a week. It can replace one of your steady rides.

Make it work for you

After warming up, do 8–10 hard efforts of 30 seconds or less, with 1–2 minutes of easy recovery between each. Keep your other rides steady.

Why this practice

You told us you want to go further with your fitness, and almost all your riding is at a steady pace. Short, hard efforts build fitness in little time.

What supports this: Milanović et al. (2015)

Sleep

02 · Consistent sleep timing

Keep your bedtime and wake time consistent every day, weekends included.

Time

No extra time. It changes when you sleep, not how long.

Make it work for you

Pick a wake-up time you can keep and a bedtime that gives you enough sleep. A slightly longer sleep at the weekend is fine; just avoid big shifts in timing.

Why this practice

You told us your wake time shifts by more than an hour at weekends. Keeping bed and wake times steady helps your body keep good time.

What supports this: Chaput et al. (2020)

The six-month journey

A Good Span is a starting point, not a fixed prescription. Each month, Claudio reflects on what’s working, adapts what isn’t and decides what comes next. New practices join one at a time, once the earlier ones feel familiar.

  1. Month 1 · Begin

    Get familiar with the first two practices and find ways to make them part of the week.

    About 30 minutes a week

  2. Month 2 · Build

    Keep the practices that are working and, when ready, add:

    • SleepMorning daylightGet outside in daylight soon after waking, for 5 minutes every morning.
    • PreventionAlcohol intakeHave one more alcohol-free day than usual each week.

    About 1 hour 5 minutes a week in all

  3. Month 3 · Refine

    Review progress and adjust the practices to fit routines, priorities and experience. If it feels right, add:

    • MovementBalancePractise balance exercises for 5 minutes, twice a week.

    About 1 hour 15 minutes a week in all, within the 2–3 hours you told us you have

  4. Months 4–5 · Deepen

    Explore the next practices in the focus areas, building on what’s been learned so far.

  5. Month 6 · Consolidate

    Identify the habits that matter most and make them easier to sustain beyond the six months.

  6. After six months · New Position

    Revisit the six dimensions to see how habits and priorities have evolved since the Starting Position, review the New Position with the Pilot and decide what to focus on next.

    Two possible next directions

    • Performance and strength could help you combine endurance, intervals and strength into a well-rounded week.
    • Sleep regularity could help you recover better between sessions, with steady timing seven days a week.

    Or keep going with what’s working. Maintaining a habit that supports wellbeing is progress too.
