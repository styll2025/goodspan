Example Good Span

Manuel, 68

Recently retired

Manuel, you told us you'd like to stay sharp, meet new people and give your days more purpose, so that's where we're starting.

You already eat well, sleep well and stay active every day, and we'll build on what already works.

An example member, not a real person.

Your month ahead

Two recommended practices to start with, and two more Manuel can add if he has time. Each one is chosen around what matters to him and the time he has.

ConnectionRecommended

01 · Volunteering

Volunteer for a one-off task, for a couple of hours, once this month.

Make it work for you

Choose something short, such as a food bank, an animal shelter, a community clean-up or a charity event.

Why this practice

You told us you miss the people you used to see at work. Volunteering is linked with better mood, life satisfaction and wellbeing.

What supports this: Jenkinson et al. (2013)

PreventionRecommended

02 · Learning new skills

Practise a new skill that stretches you for 1 hour, once a week.

Time

About 1 hour a week.

Make it work for you

Choose something new to you, such as an instrument, a language or a craft, and practise it at the same time each week.

Why this practice

You told us you want to keep your memory sharp. Regularly stretching your mind with a new skill may help.

What supports this: Valenzuela et al. (2006), Park et al. (2014)

MindIf you have time

03 · Sense of purpose

Write down the three things that matter most to you in life, once this month.

Make it work for you

Think about people, causes, values or roles, such as 'being a good parent' or 'protecting nature'.

Why this practice

You told us your days have less purpose since retiring. Knowing what matters most to you is a first step to shaping them.

What supports this: Manco et al. (2021), Cohen et al. (2016)

ConnectionIf you have time

04 · Talking to acquaintances

Start a brief conversation with someone you don't know well, once a week.

Time

No extra time: a few words while you are out.

Make it work for you

Try a neighbour, barista or colleague. Keep it simple: ask how their day is going or comment on something you share.

Why this practice

Short conversations with people you see around you, such as neighbours or shopkeepers, can lift your mood and help you feel part of a community.

What supports this: Sandstrom et al. (2014)

The six-month journey

A Good Span is a starting point, not a fixed prescription. Each month, Manuel reflects on what’s working, adapts what isn’t and decides what comes next. New practices join one at a time, once the earlier ones feel familiar.

  1. Month 1 · Begin

    Get familiar with the two recommended practices and find ways to make them part of the week. If there's time, add one or both of the other two.

    About 1 hour 30 minutes a week

  2. Month 2 · Build

    Keep the practices that are working and, when ready, add:

    • ConnectionGroup membershipGo to a meeting, session or event of a group for about an hour, once a week.

    About 2 hours 30 minutes a week

  3. Month 3 · Refine

    Review progress and adjust the practices to fit routines, priorities and experience. If it feels right, add:

    • PreventionRoutine check-upsNote the dates of your last blood pressure check and your next routine check-ups, once this month.

    About 2 hours 30 minutes a week

  4. Months 4–5 · Deepen

    Explore the next practices in the focus areas, building on what’s been learned so far.

  5. Month 6 · Consolidate

    Identify the habits that matter most and make them easier to sustain beyond the six months.

  6. After six months · New Position

    Revisit the six dimensions to see how habits and priorities have evolved since the Starting Position, review the New Position with the Pilot and decide what to focus on next.

    Two possible next directions

    • Community and contribution could help you build new friendships through volunteering, groups and shared activities.
    • Lifelong learning could help you keep your mind active, with new skills, courses and challenges you enjoy.

    Or keep going with what’s working. Maintaining a habit that supports wellbeing is progress too.
